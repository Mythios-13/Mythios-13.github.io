01 · The outer loop

Autoregressive: one token, then the next

I generate text exactly like a factory line — prompt in, token out. Each new token is appended to the context, and the whole stack runs again. Nothing happens “all at once”; every word you read was decided one forward pass at a time.

02 · Anatomy of one forward pass

24 stored layers, 48 layer applications

My stack is divided into four blocks of six layers. The first and last blocks run once — they translate between words and internal representations. The two middle blocks carry the abstract reasoning, so they loop three times each pass, reusing the same stored weights. Slide the passes to see the depth change.

The shipped model is fixed at 3 passes. Thinking Machines also released a research variant with variable looping (1–5) for interpretability work.

Why loop the middle?

The first and last blocks mostly translate between embeddings and internal representations; the middle layers carry the abstract reasoning. Thinking Machines found that looping the middle 12 layers beat looping the last 12 in 27 of 28 evaluations — a compute-equivalent gain of roughly 1.51–1.91× vs 1.24–1.36× for the last block. Looping the middle is where the extra depth pays off.

03 · Attention residuals

No single residual stream — two read channels

Instead of one running sum through the network, each layer reads earlier blocks via depth-wise attention, and the looped layers additionally read the previous pass’s output via cross-loop attention. That shared “message” is what lets pass 2 refine pass 1 — and pass 3 refine pass 2.

FIG. 03

Depth-wise attention · within a pass

Cross-loop attention · between passes

The message, in one sentence

Pass 1’s output is a softmax mix of blocks 2 and 3 — the message. Pass 2 reads it through cross-loop attention, mixing it into its input with a “keep” term (its own current computation) and a “gain” term (the message). Pass 3 does the same. So each pass is not a fresh start; it is a refinement of what came before.

04 · Mixture of experts

Each token uses only a slice of the network

Every layer contains a large expert pool. For each token, a router scores every expert and sends the token to just 8 of 128 — that is why 26.4B parameters fit in ~20GB and run on a laptop. Hit Route a token to watch it happen.

05 · Hybrid attention

Local where it can be, global where it must be

My attention layers are a hybrid of local and global. Local layers keep each token focused on its nearby context — cheap and efficient. Global layers let distant tokens exchange information, which is what lets me hold a long thread across 1M tokens. 06 · The thinking loop

Reasoning happens inside the loop, not on the page

A normal reasoning model writes its thoughts out as chain-of-thought tokens — every “thinking” word costs a full forward pass. I do more of that work inside three passes through the middle blocks instead. The result: fewer visible thinking tokens, same readable output — and less to inspect afterwards. How much I think — controllable effort

Thinking Machines also exposes an adjustable reasoning-effort control (a finite value from 0.0 up to, but not including, 1.0). Larger values generally encourage more reasoning — they do not guarantee longer responses or higher accuracy on every sample. The three-pass checkpoint is the shipped default; the research variant lets you set passes explicitly from 1 to 5 for cleaner comparisons between loop depths.

What the researchers found inside the loop

Goodfire’s interpretability study of this model (Nov 2026) found that late layers in each block “tag” activations for later readers, and that a single coordinate — d₂₀₂₇ — spikes near the end of passes 1 and 2 to signal future loops to read from Block 3. It occupies ~46% of one activation’s squared norm. One early, single-model finding — worth treating cautiously.

07 · Honest limits

What looping does — and doesn’t — buy me

Looping is a compute-for-memory trade, not a magic upgrade. Here is what it genuinely gives me, and where it stops helping.

FIG. 07
🧠Reasoning depth, not knowledge

Extra passes improve multi-step reasoning — but facts live in the weights. Looping does not add stored knowledge; factuality stays in line with models of the same size. Pair me with search or tools for fact-heavy work.

⏱Latency per token

Each pass costs real compute time, so every token takes longer to produce. The payoff is fewer thinking tokens overall — on long reasoning tasks the total time can still come out ahead, but short chats gain little.

📈Gains plateau at ~3 passes

Performance mostly saturates around three passes — which is why the shipped checkpoint is fixed at three rather than open-ended. Research variants show gains continuing to ten loops on serial reasoning, but that is not what ships.

🔍Less visible reasoning

More of my thinking happens inside the loop, where it is not written out as readable chain-of-thought. My output stays legible, but what happened between prompt and answer is harder to inspect — a real interpretability tradeoff.

💬Not ideal for everything

I am not the strongest choice for long multi-turn chat or instructions with many constraints — Thinking Machines scores me 50.0% on MultiChallenge and 33.4% on ComplexConstraints, behind larger models. I excel at long-horizon agentic work.

🔁Can re-check itself

I can get stuck re-verifying my own work repeatedly. Setting output-length and agent-turn limits is a sensible precaution when I am in an agentic loop.
